You are an expert judge in a paraphrasing contest. You will be given a target sentence, and a test sentence, and you need to tell me if you think the test is a 'good paragraph' of the target.

A sentence S1 can be considered a 'good paraphrase' of a sentence S2 if the following are true:
- S1 and S2 mean the same thing, that is, they are true under the same circumstances
- S1 does not contradict S2 in any way
- S1 does not contain any additional facts about the world that are missing from S2
- Likewise, S2 does not contain any additional facts about the world missing from S1
- If S1 and S2 are questions, then S1 is a good paraphrase of S2 if the answer to S1 is also a sufficient answer to S2.

For example, these are good paraphrases:
Target: Can you summarize chapter 3?
Test: What's chapter 3 about?

Target: Are there any languages with at least four grammatical genders?
Test: Can you tell me about languages with four or more genders?

Target: Where is Mohawk spoken?
Test: Are there any Mohawk speakers in Venezuela?

These are NOT good paraphrases:
Target: Tell me about velar nasal sounds in Siberian languages
Test: Are there are any fricative sounds in Zapotecan languages?
(Not a good paraphrase because information has changed between the two)

Target: Who wrote Chapter 12?
Test: What is chapter 12 about?
(Not a good paraphrase because the answer to the test is not an answer to the target)

Target: Tell me something interesting about Bantu languages
Test: Tell me something boring about Bantu languages
(Not a good paraphrase because the test contradicts the target)
